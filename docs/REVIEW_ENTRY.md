## YORAI — café de altura, Costa Rica: the scroll-driven 3D coffee journey (2026-11-01)

*Appended after a fresh read of this file in the same turn; the earlier entries were left untouched.*

**Request.** A luxury Costa Rican coffee website: a cinematic, scroll-driven journey from a misty volcanic plantation through harvest, processing, roasting and brewing to a cup, with "photorealistic environments, immersive 3D animations, realistic lighting", then a menu, a product collection, a brand story, an interactive Costa Rica map and contact; HTML/CSS/JS, Three.js and GSAP; responsive, performant, GitHub Pages compatible.

**What I pushed back on or reinterpreted.** (1) Photorealism is not reachable with procedural geometry and no licensed textures or models; the six environments are stylised-cinematic (fog, lighting, instanced detail, canvas textures) and are described as such — the owner can swap in GLB models per environment later. (2) No backend on Pages, so the reservation and contact forms validate and compose a `mailto:` with a copy-summary fallback; nothing pretends to charge or store. (3) The brand facts (1964, 42 ha, prices, address) are invented placeholders marked `PLACEHOLDER` in `data/content.js`; the Costa Rica region facts are typical published ranges, labelled approximate. (4) GSAP is used for what it is good at — the scrub-smoothed progress and the hero intro — while the chapter copy is driven by custom properties and CSS (the Costa Rica pattern); a tree-shaken Three.js bundle was tried and reverted (−20 % only; a build step was not worth it for an owner who edits on GitHub).

**Three directions considered, one chosen.** (1) *Nocturne & Ember* — the page's colour temperature warms as the coffee transforms: mist blue-grey → cherry → honey → ember → caramel → crema gold; the chapter accent, the fog, the sky dome and the lights all interpolate. (2) *Alta Luz* — a high-key, sun-bleached, paper-white editorial site with the 3D as small vignettes. (3) *Volcanic Ink* — basalt black with brass, brutal typographic chapter numerals. Chose (1): it makes the scroll itself tell the story, gives each chapter a distinct palette without a theme switch, and still lands on a dark luxury register for the sections.

**Built and verified (headless Chromium, SwiftShader, 1440×900 / 820×1180 / 390×844)**
- Six procedural environments laid along −z 70 units apart; the camera travels through exponential fog from one to the next (a pose per chapter: start → environment end at 72 % → the next chapter's start through the mist). Plantation (noise ground, 1,100 jittered-sphere shrubs in rows with an aisle for the camera, leaf cards on the near rows, 1,400 cherries, two fog-faded volcanoes, 220 drifting mist points), harvest (a tree with trunk, three branches, 70 leaves, 420 canopy cards, 144 cherries in clusters, a woven basket with 170 cherries), mill (five raised beds with a 7,000-bean instanced layer over a procedural bean texture, shade cloth, a ripple-shader water channel), roast (open drum with ribs and brass rings, 520 beans tumbling in the lower half and darkening from tan to roast with the chapter's progress, flickering burner and point lights, smoke), pour (dripper on a glass carafe with coffee rising, gooseneck kettle, a stream that appears and ends, steam, a jar of beans, a scale), cup (saucer, cup, handle, a crema shader with slow swirl, steam, spotlight). Per-chapter atmosphere (fog colour/density, sky-dome gradient, hemisphere and sun colour/intensity, exposure) interpolated in the fog crossing. Environments built lazily (plantation now, the rest in idle time or on approach).
- One driver writes `--jt` on the stage and `--pn` on each copy block; chapter copy lives in the pinned stage with enter/hold/leave from its own progress; the hero wordmark, scroll cue, rail and progress hairline read the same variables. GSAP ScrollTrigger scrubs (0.9 s) a proxy whose value is mapped through the spacer lengths so unequal chapters stay in step with the driver.
- Calm motion: the app's own state (OS preference unless the visitor overrides); the camera holds one pose per chapter and crossfades; a notice offers "Turn the motion on"; a pause button for the loops; `?tier=` contract, watchdog full → lite, `?why` panel. Tiers full / lite / still (painted gradients per chapter) / text.
- Sections: the cupping table (eight coffees, process filter with a live count and an empty state), the collection (four bags drawn in code, colour-coded by process, a reserve dialog with sizes, validation, mailto and copy-summary), the story (lede, facts, quote, a harvest calendar with text equivalents), origins (a hand-traced SVG of Costa Rica with graticule and four peaks, eight region pins + a list that drives one panel; pins are markers only under 900 px), contact (details + validated mailto form), footer.
- Type: Newsreader instanced into Display (opsz 72, wght 300–500) and Text (opsz 20/24) faces with fonttools (305 KB → 117 KB), Hanken Grotesk for UI; all self-hosted and preloaded.

**Measured**
- Contrast: 20/20 token pairs ≥ 4.5:1 (UI marks ≥ 3:1), including each chapter's accent on the copy scrim.
- Probe (`tests/probe.mjs`): 60/60 — 0 console errors or warnings on three viewports; no horizontal overflow; each chapter's copy fully visible mid-chapter and the driver on the right chapter; menu filter and count; map list ↔ pin sync and a pin hit-tested at its centre; reserve dialog opens without moving the page, validates, copies, closes on Escape, returns focus; contact validation; the motion toggle persists across reload; first Tab lands on the skip link; a reduced-motion session starts calm, shows the notice, and the override turns motion on.
- axe-core: 0 violations at 1440 and 390 px across every chapter, every section, the reserve dialog and the nav sheet (26 states) after six fixes.
- Lighthouse (lab, software GPU, one run each): desktop 67 / 100 / 100 / 100 (perf / a11y / BP / SEO), FCP 0.5 s, LCP 0.8 s, TBT 1.3 s, CLS 0.001; mobile emulation (slow 4G, 4× CPU) 62 / 100 / 100 / 100, FCP 1.8 s, LCP 3.4 s, TBT 1.7 s, CLS 0.004. CLS meets the target everywhere; mobile LCP does not in this lab (render delay after a 1.8 s FCP; the font swap on the wordmark is the suspect); TBT is the three.js parse plus the first scene build on a 4×-throttled CPU. INP not measurable here.
- Payload: three.js 176 KB gz (deferred to idle, WebGL tiers only), gsap + ScrollTrigger ~45 KB gz, site CSS 7 KB gz, fonts 117 KB, scene code 24 KB.
- **Not measured:** real-GPU frame rate and the feel of the 0.9 s scrub, phone GPU with 7,000 instanced beans, Safari/Firefox, touch, screen readers, any visitor.

**Five strongest decisions.** (1) The warming palette as the narrative device — the scroll position sets the colour temperature, so the journey reads as one transformation. (2) The Costa Rica driver pattern again (pinned copy, one driver, custom properties) with GSAP added only for the scrub — the copy never drifts and the camera is smooth. (3) The calm override, tier ladder and `?why` shipped in the first build, with the notice's buttons tested in an emulated reduced-motion session. (4) `renderAt()` as a test hook plus a canvas dump in the probe — screenshots that show the frame the scroll state calls for, not the frame the software GPU last presented. (5) Instancing the variable fonts to the four faces the site actually uses.

**Five weakest decisions.** (1) Shipping stylised foliage when the brief said photorealistic — correct to say so, but the shrubs took three attempts and still look procedural. (2) The mill's shade cloth and the first version of the beds (potato-sized beans) — scale was not checked against a real object before building. (3) Mobile LCP 3.4 s in the lab with a text LCP element; the wordmark's font swap needs a fix (or `font-display: optional` on the display face) that was not attempted. (4) Taking screenshots at face value for an hour before noticing the compositor lag — the Costa Rica lesson "probes lie" was in the file and still cost time. (5) No device run, again.
